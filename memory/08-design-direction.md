# Design direction, mostly in the owner's words

Keeping the exact phrasing because the standard is easier to hit when you can
hear it.

- "enterprise UX, not vibecoded" — clean, outcome-driven, no decoration that
  does not encode something true.
- "it should be much more actionable and insightful and isplayed in good way"
  about the Commitments screen.
- "make this page much more cleaner and of enterprise level UI".
- On littlebird: "look how clean ux does littlebird has, i want something like
  this", immediately followed by "dont copy paste the entire littlebird's ui
  brainstorm and make it more better".
- On a draft that was too sparse: "add more informative things it looks very
  short and concise rn".
- On two drafts that missed: "this looks weird / bad", and "no man create
  something good, i dont like this. create something different and cool even
  idk what but something diff".
- On the one that landed: "i think i really like this new ux, start building
  it".

Five iterations were needed. The ones that failed were either a copy of
somebody else's interface or a dense dashboard. The one that worked was a
single idea stated plainly: memory that arrives where you are working.

## Rules that came out of it

- Say the thing in a sentence a person would say. "Enterprise $499/mo became
  Enterprise contact us" beats a diff view.
- Speak only with something specific. A card that says nothing is worse than no
  card, which is why a first visit shows nothing at all.
- Structure must encode something true. Numbered steps only for real sequences;
  a chip only where state matters.
- Names on screen are the user's names: "Top screens", not "Projects"; "Active
  time", not "Focused time"; "Time per day", not "Daily rhythm". Each of those
  was a correction from the owner.
- The Agents page was removed outright. MCP still works; review lives in
  Commitments and the Audit log.
